Surfaces, Partial Derivatives & the Tangent Plane

Consider the quadratic surface $$ z = f(x,y) = a\,x^2 + b\,y^2 $$ Its partial derivatives are $$ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 2ax, \qquad \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 2by $$ and at a point $(x_0, y_0)$ the tangent plane to the surface is $$ z \approx f(x_0,y_0) + \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x_0,y_0)\,(x-x_0) + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x_0,y_0)\,(y-y_0) $$

Use the sliders to change $a$, $b$, and the point $(x_0,y_0)$. The orange plane is the tangent plane at the marked point — notice how it always touches the surface at exactly that point and tilts to match its slope in both directions.

Drag to rotate · scroll or pinch to zoom · double-click to reset the view.

Try it: set $a$ and $b$ to opposite signs (e.g. $a=1$, $b=-1$). The origin becomes a saddle point: rotate the view to see the surface curve upward along one axis and downward along the other, while the tangent plane at the origin is exactly flat (horizontal), since $\partial f/\partial x = \partial f/\partial y = 0$ there.
